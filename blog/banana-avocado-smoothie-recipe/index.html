Banana Avocado Smoothie Recipe (Creamy & Filling)

If you want a banana avocado smoothie that actually keeps you full, this is the one. Banana and strawberries carry the flavour, peanut butter adds richness, and two full cups of spinach disappear into the fruit so completely you will forget the greens are there. It is creamy, filling, naturally sweet — and ready in five minutes with one blender.

The Recipe: Nutty Berry Prospect

5 MINUTES · 1 LARGE GLASS · 1 BLENDER

Simple ingredients, generous flavour: banana and strawberries carry this blend, with spinach keeping it fresh and green. The greens all but disappear into the fruit, which makes this a kind first step if green smoothies still feel new to you.

Ingredients

  • 1 large frozen banana
  • 3 large frozen strawberries
  • ½ cup frozen blueberries
  • 2 cups fresh baby spinach
  • 1 cup filtered water
  • 1 tbsp peanut butter

Method

  1. Pour in the liquid first, add the greens and soft fresh ingredients, and blend 20–30 seconds until the greens vanish.
  2. Add the frozen fruit, powders, seeds and the rest. Blend until thick and creamy, pausing once to push the mixture down.
  3. Taste, then adjust: more liquid if too thick, more frozen fruit if too thin. Pour and enjoy straight away.
Swap: Almond butter for peanut butter.
Nutrition per glass (estimated): ~285 cal · Protein 8g · Carbs 51g · Fiber 9g · Fat 9g · Natural sugars ~27g
High in vitamin K · High in vitamin C · High in vitamin A

Why It Works

This banana avocado smoothie style blend works because it balances all three macros in one glass. The banana and berries deliver fast-acting carbohydrates and natural sugars for immediate energy, while the peanut butter contributes healthy fats and a little protein that slow digestion — so the energy release is steady instead of spiky.

The two cups of spinach are the quiet hero. Spinach is rich in vitamin K, vitamin A, folate and iron, yet its mild flavour vanishes entirely behind the fruit. You get a meaningful serving of leafy greens without the "green smoothie" taste that puts beginners off. With 9g of fibre per glass, this blend genuinely satisfies — most people find it holds them for 3–4 hours as a breakfast replacement.

Using frozen fruit instead of fresh (plus no ice needed) is what gives this smoothie its thick, milkshake-like texture. Frozen banana in particular is the secret to creaminess without any dairy — it blends into a soft-serve consistency that makes the whole glass feel indulgent.

Tips & Variations

Make it richer: Add half a ripe avocado for extra creaminess and healthy monounsaturated fats — this is what turns it into a true banana avocado smoothie. The flavour stays fruity; the texture becomes luxurious.

Boost the protein: Add a scoop of vanilla protein powder or a tablespoon of hemp seeds to push protein above 15g — ideal after a workout.

Lower the sugar: Swap the banana for half a banana plus half a cup of frozen cauliflower (you will not taste it) to cut natural sugars while keeping the thickness.

Meal prep: Portion the frozen fruit and spinach into freezer bags. In the morning, dump a bag in the blender, add water and peanut butter, and blend — under two minutes from freezer to glass.

FAQ

Is banana avocado smoothie good for weight loss?

It can be — avocado and peanut butter add healthy fats that keep you full for hours, which means fewer snacks later. At around 285 calories with 9g of fibre, this banana avocado smoothie works well as a meal replacement.

Can you taste the spinach in this smoothie?

Barely. The banana and strawberries completely overpower the spinach flavour — you get the nutrients and the green colour without a leafy taste. It is an ideal first green smoothie.

Can I make this without peanut butter?

Yes — swap in almond butter, or leave it out and add half an avocado for the same creamy richness. The book suggests almond butter as the direct swap.

Is this smoothie good before a workout?

Absolutely. With 51g of carbs for energy, 8g of protein and natural sugars from fruit, it makes an excellent pre-workout fuel about 45–60 minutes before exercise.
