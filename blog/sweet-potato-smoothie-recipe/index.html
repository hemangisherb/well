Sweet Potato Smoothie (Surprisingly Delicious)

This sweet potato smoothie is the recipe that surprises everyone who tries it. Sweet potato in a smoothie sounds strange until the first sip — then it makes complete sense. Baked, chilled overnight and blended with almond milk, cinnamon, turmeric and almond butter, it turns into a thick, creamy shake, warm and gently spiced, more like dessert than a vegetable drink. From The Ultimate Smoothie Wellness Guide — recipe № 68, Spicy Sweet Potato Shake — five minutes, one blender.

There is no banana, no mango, no pile of fruit doing the heavy lifting here. The sweet potato carries the whole drink: natural sweetness, body and a deep orange colour, with the spices doing the flavour work. The Recipe

5 MINUTES · 1 LARGE GLASS · 1 BLENDER

A bright, easy blend built around sweet potato. Gently sweet rather than sugary, it keeps the fruit in check and lets the savoury, herbal notes come through.

Ingredients

  • 1¼ cups almond milk
  • ⅔–1 cup cooked mashed chilled sweet potato
  • ¼ tsp turmeric
  • ¾ tsp cinnamon
  • 2 ice cubes
  • 1 tbsp almond butter

Method

  1. Use sweet potato that you have baked, peeled and chilled until cold — warm potato will not blend into a shake.
  2. Add the almond butter, turmeric, cinnamon and ice cubes. Blend until thick and creamy, pausing once to push the mixture down.
  3. Taste, then adjust: more liquid if too thick, more sweet potato if too thin. Pour and enjoy straight away.

Swap: Use cooked mashed pumpkin instead of sweet potato.

Nutrition per glass (estimated): ~285 cal · Protein 8g · Carbs 41g · Fibre 9g · Fat 12g · Natural sugars ~11g

Why It Works

Sweet potato is a surprisingly good smoothie ingredient. It is rich in beta-carotene, which your body turns into vitamin A — the nutrient behind healthy skin, good vision and a strong immune system — and the book flags this blend as high in vitamin A, calcium and fibre. The fibre is the quiet hero: 9g per glass slows digestion, steadies blood sugar and keeps you full for hours, which is why this recipe sits in the Lose Weight chapter.

The spices are not decoration. Cinnamon adds warmth and sweetness without sugar, and it is one of the few spices with real evidence for steadying blood sugar after a meal. Turmeric brings curcumin, the compound behind its golden colour and its reputation for supporting joints. A quarter of a teaspoon colours the glass gold and adds a gentle earthy note without turning bitter.

Then there is the almond butter. One tablespoon adds healthy fats that help your body absorb the fat-soluble vitamins in the sweet potato and turmeric, and it rounds out the texture so the shake feels rich rather than thin. Together the six ingredients land at about 285 calories — a light breakfast or an afternoon snack that actually satisfies.

Tips & Variations

Bake and chill ahead: This is the one step you cannot skip. Bake a sweet potato the evening before, peel, mash and chill it — warm potato will not blend into a shake. Cooked sweet potato keeps for three to four days, so bake two or three at once.

Control the thickness: The recipe gives a range — two-thirds to one cup of sweet potato — and that range is your thickness dial. Use two-thirds of a cup for a lighter, drinkable shake, or a full cup for something thick enough to eat with a spoon. Add an extra ice cube if you like it frostier.

Try the pumpkin swap: The book's own note says to use cooked mashed pumpkin instead of sweet potato, and it works beautifully — same method, same spices, a slightly lighter result. A perfect autumn variation when pumpkins are cheap.

Adjust the spice to taste: Three-quarters of a teaspoon of cinnamon is generous, and it should be — cinnamon is what makes this taste like a treat. If you are new to turmeric, start with a pinch and build up to the full quarter teaspoon.

FAQ

Does a sweet potato smoothie actually taste good?

Yes — and that is the surprise. Baked sweet potato is gently sweet rather than sugary, and with cinnamon, turmeric and almond butter it tastes warm and spiced, more like a dessert shake than a vegetable drink. At ~285 calories it is light enough for breakfast.

Can I use raw sweet potato in a smoothie?

No. The recipe depends on sweet potato baked, peeled and chilled until cold — warm potato will not blend into a shake, and raw potato will not blend smooth at all. Bake one the evening before and chill it, ready to scoop into the blender in the morning.

Can I make this sweet potato smoothie ahead of time?

It is best drunk straight away, but it keeps in a sealed jar in the fridge for up to 24 hours — give it a good shake before drinking, as the sweet potato settles. The baked sweet potato itself keeps for three to four days, so you can prep several mornings at once.

Is a sweet potato smoothie good for weight loss?

This one is — it sits in the Lose Weight chapter for a reason. At roughly 285 calories with 9g of fibre and only about 11g of natural sugars, it is filling without being heavy, and the fibre and almond butter keep you satisfied for hours.
