Banana Protein Smoothie for Morning Energy

A good banana protein smoothie should do two things before 9am: wake you up and keep you full until lunch. This one does both. Frozen banana and mango give it a thick, milkshake texture and quick morning fuel, strawberries add brightness, and three stalks of celery fold in so gently you taste the fruit long before the greens. It comes from The Ultimate Smoothie Wellness Guide — recipe № 7, The Celery Nutcase, from the Energy & Strength chapter — and it takes five minutes and one blender.

As written, the blend carries 7g of protein and around 340 calories, with 10g of fibre and a big hit of vitamin C and potassium — a genuine meal replacement, not a sweet snack. And if you want it to work harder as a protein breakfast, one scoop of protein powder takes it to roughly 25g without changing the flavour at all. That is the whole point of this recipe: a sweet, creamy banana smoothie that actually carries you through the morning.

The Recipe

5 MINUTES · 1 LARGE GLASS · 1 BLENDER

This one leads with mango and banana, with celery folded in so gently you notice the flavour long before the greens. It is made to keep you full: the creamy base, fibre and healthy fats mean it works as a proper breakfast or a satisfying lunch when time is short.

Ingredients

  • 4 large frozen strawberries
  • 1 frozen banana
  • 3 large fresh celery stalks
  • 1 cup frozen mango
  • 1 cup filtered water
  • 1 tbsp almond butter

Method

  1. Pour in the liquid first, add the greens and soft fresh ingredients, and blend 20–30 seconds until the greens vanish.
  2. Add the frozen fruit, powders, seeds and the rest. Blend until thick and creamy, pausing once to push the mixture down.
  3. Taste, then adjust: more liquid if too thick, more frozen fruit if too thin. Pour and enjoy straight away.
Nutrition per glass (estimated): ~340 cal · Protein 7g · Carbs 64g · Fibre 10g · Fat 10g · Natural sugars ~42g
High in vitamin C · High in fibre · High in potassium

Why It Works

Morning energy is not about caffeine — it is about fuel that arrives fast and then lasts. Banana and mango are the fast part: natural sugars and easily digested carbohydrate that your body can use within minutes of drinking, which is why this smoothie feels like it works almost immediately. The lasting part comes from the other half of the glass. Ten grams of fibre slows the whole thing down, so the sugars release steadily instead of spiking and crashing, and the almond butter adds healthy fats that stretch the energy out over hours rather than minutes.

Celery earns its place quietly. It is mostly water, so it hydrates you first thing — most of us wake up mildly dehydrated, which is one reason mornings feel sluggish — and it brings potassium, which helps muscles and nerves work properly. You would never guess it was there from the taste: the mango leads, the banana thickens, the strawberries lift it, and the celery just disappears into the background.

At roughly 340 calories it sits exactly where a breakfast should — enough to count as a meal, light enough not to slow you down. Drink it as your breakfast on busy mornings, or an hour before a workout, when you want fuel that will not sit heavily.

Tips & Variations

Boost the protein: Add one scoop of protein powder with the frozen fruit — pea, soy, whey or hemp all work — and this becomes a roughly 25g-protein breakfast with no change in flavour. Add a splash more water if the powder thickens it too much.

Freeze the banana properly: Peel ripe bananas, break them in halves and freeze them flat in a bag. A frozen banana is what makes this thick and cold like a milkshake; a fresh one will come out thin and warm, and no amount of ice quite fixes it.

Swap the greens: If celery is not your thing, a big handful of spinach vanishes into this blend completely — the mango hides both the colour and the taste — and it adds iron and folate for free. The easiest swap in the whole book.

Make it nut-free: Swap the almond butter for sunflower seed butter and nothing else changes. The recipe uses water, not milk, so that one swap makes the whole glass nut-free.

Prep it the night before: Bag the frozen fruit and chopped celery together in the freezer, and keep the almond butter and water by the blender. In the morning it is a two-minute job — tip, blend, pour.

FAQ

Is this banana protein smoothie actually high in protein?

As written in the book, this blend carries 7g of protein per glass — modest, but it is a real breakfast, not a sugar hit. Add one scoop of your favourite protein powder and it jumps to around 25g, which is a proper protein breakfast. Pea, soy, whey and hemp powders all blend in easily.

Can I make this banana protein smoothie without celery?

Yes. The celery is deliberately gentle here — you taste the mango and banana first — but if you would rather skip it, use a big handful of spinach instead, or leave the greens out entirely. The smoothie stays thick and sweet either way.

Can I use fresh strawberries instead of frozen?

Yes — use 4 large fresh strawberries and add a small handful of ice cubes so it still turns out thick and cold. Frozen fruit is what gives this blend its milkshake texture, so fresh fruit alone will come out thinner.

Is a banana protein smoothie good before a workout?

Yes, it is a good pre-workout breakfast. Banana and mango give you quick, easily digested carbohydrate for fuel, celery adds water and potassium, and the almond butter slows everything down just enough that the energy lasts. Drink it 45–60 minutes before training, and add the protein scoop afterwards if you want the recovery benefit too.
