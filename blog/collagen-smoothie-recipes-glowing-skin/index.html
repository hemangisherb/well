The Recipe

5 MINUTES · 1 LARGE GLASS · 1 BLENDER

Simple ingredients, generous flavour: raspberries and goji. It is made to keep you full: the creamy base, fibre and healthy fats mean it works as a proper breakfast or a satisfying lunch when time is short.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup almond milk
  • ⅓ cup goji berries
  • ½ to 1 cup frozen raspberries
  • 1 scoop dairy-free protein powder
  • 1 tbsp almond butter

Method

  1. Pour in the liquid first, add the greens and soft fresh ingredients, and blend 20–30 seconds until the greens vanish.
  2. Add the frozen fruit, powders, seeds and the rest. Blend until thick and creamy, pausing once to push the mixture down.
  3. Taste, then adjust: more liquid if too thick, more frozen fruit if too thin. Pour and enjoy straight away.
Nutrition per glass (estimated): ~405 cal · Protein 32g · Carbs 40g · Fibre 13g · Fat 14g · Natural sugars ~22g
High in fibre · High in calcium · High in vitamin C

Why It Works

Collagen is the protein that keeps skin firm, springy and smooth, and your body cannot make it without two things: vitamin C and amino acids from protein. Goji berries are one of the richest natural sources of vitamin C, and raspberries add more, along with deep-red antioxidants that help protect skin from the free radicals behind dullness and early lines. The protein powder supplies the amino acids — 32g in a single glass, more than most people get from a cooked breakfast.

But a glow smoothie that leaves you hungry by mid-morning is no good to anyone, and that is where this recipe is smarter than most. The almond butter adds healthy fats that support the skin barrier and help your body absorb the fat-soluble vitamins in the berries, while 13g of fibre slows digestion and steadies blood sugar — and steady blood sugar means fewer of the spikes that show up on skin. At roughly 405 calories it is a genuine meal replacement, not a snack in disguise.

It sits in the Anti-Aging & Glow chapter for a reason: every ingredient earns its place for skin. Drink it most mornings for a few weeks and the change is the quiet kind — skin that looks a little brighter, a little less tired — which is exactly how real nutrition works.

Tips & Variations

Soak the goji berries first: Dried goji berries blend much smoother after ten minutes in warm water — drain them, then add them with the liquid. Skip the soak and they can stay a little chewy; the texture is noticeably silkier with it.

Start with half a cup of raspberries: Half a cup keeps it creamy and sweet; a full cup makes it thicker, tangier and a deeper pink. Taste after the first blend and add more if you want it sharper.

Make it greener: A handful of spinach disappears completely into this blend — the raspberries hide both the colour and the taste — and it adds iron and folate for free. The easiest upgrade in the whole book, and it costs nothing in flavour.

Swap the protein: The book's own note says to use any protein powder you like — pea, soy and hemp all work, and collagen peptides blend in just as easily. For a nut-free version, use oat milk and sunflower seed butter — the berries still carry the flavour.

FAQ

Do collagen smoothies actually help your skin?

Your body builds its own collagen from vitamin C and amino acids, and this blend delivers both: goji berries and raspberries are rich in vitamin C, and the protein powder supplies 32g of protein per glass. No smoothie transforms skin overnight, but a daily blend gives your body the raw materials it needs.

Can I use fresh raspberries instead of frozen?

Yes — use 1 cup of fresh raspberries and add a small handful of ice cubes so it still turns out thick and cold. Start with half a cup and add more if you like it thicker and tangier.

What protein powder works best in a collagen smoothie?

The book uses one scoop of dairy-free protein powder, and any kind works — pea, soy or hemp. A scoop of collagen peptides blends in just as easily, and the book's own swap note says to use any other protein powder instead.

Can I make this collagen smoothie nut-free?

Yes. Swap the almond milk for oat milk and the almond butter for sunflower seed butter. The texture stays creamy and the goji berries and raspberries still carry the flavour — the nuts are there for healthy fats and richness, not for the taste.
