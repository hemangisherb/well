1. Spinach Meets Cinnamon (Full Free Recipe)

5 MINUTES · 1 LARGE GLASS · 1 BLENDER

Built for flavour first, this blend puts banana and coconut centre stage, with spinach and celery adding colour, fibre and a clean finish. Unapologetically green, this is for days when you want a full serving of leafy flavour and do not mind tasting it.

Ingredients

  • 1¼ cups almond coconut milk
  • 1–2 stalks of celery
  • 1 handful of spinach
  • 2 pitted dates
  • 1 frozen banana
  • ½ tsp cinnamon

Method

  1. Pour in the liquid first, add the greens and soft fresh ingredients, and blend 20–30 seconds until the greens vanish.
  2. Add the frozen fruit, powders, seeds and the rest. Blend until thick and creamy, pausing once to push the mixture down.
  3. Taste, then adjust: more liquid if too thick, more frozen fruit if too thin. Pour and enjoy straight away.

Why Low Calorie Smoothies Work for Weight Loss

Low calorie smoothie recipes work for weight loss because they solve the two problems that sink most diets: hunger and boredom. A 150–350 calorie smoothie delivers real food volume — fibre from fruit, greens and seeds plus plenty of water — which fills the stomach and signals fullness far more effectively than a 300-calorie pastry. You eat less at the next meal because the smoothie genuinely satisfied you.

The fibre is the quiet hero here. Each of these five blends carries 6–12g of fibre per glass, and fibre slows digestion, steadies blood sugar and keeps hunger away for hours. The greens also bring the micronutrients that keep energy up during a calorie deficit — vitamin K, vitamin C, potassium and folate — so you lose weight without feeling depleted.

The other half of the formula is consistency. Every recipe here leads with flavour — banana and coconut in Spinach Meets Cinnamon, berries in Polka Dot Berry Dance — and lets the greens work quietly in the background. A weight-loss smoothie you look forward to becomes a daily habit, and daily habits are what move the scale.

Tips & Variations

Use them as meal replacements, not additions: A low calorie smoothie only helps weight loss when it replaces a higher-calorie meal — typically breakfast or lunch. Drinking one on top of your normal meals just adds calories.

Add protein when you need staying power: If a 200-calorie blend leaves you hungry by 11am, add the book’s optional boost — protein powder, hemp seeds, chia seeds or a spoon of nut butter. It adds calories, but it buys you hours of fullness, which is the trade that matters.

Keep the fruit honest: Dates and bananas make smoothies taste like dessert, but they are calorie-dense. Two dates and one banana is plenty per glass — exactly what the featured recipe uses. More fruit means more sugar, even the natural kind.

Freeze everything: Frozen banana and berries give thick, milkshake texture without ice, which waters down flavour. Keep bags in the freezer and you are two minutes from a weight-loss breakfast.

FAQ

What is the best low calorie smoothie for weight loss?

The best low calorie smoothie keeps you full on few calories: greens for volume, fibre from fruit and seeds, and a touch of healthy fat. Our Spinach Meets Cinnamon — spinach, celery, banana, dates and cinnamon — is a proven weight-loss blend from The Ultimate Smoothie Wellness Guide.

How many calories should a weight loss smoothie have?

A weight loss smoothie works best as a light meal or snack replacement at roughly 150–350 calories. Four of the five blends above sit under 250 calories, so they slot easily into a calorie deficit without leaving you hungry.

Can I drink a low calorie smoothie every day?

Yes — a daily low calorie smoothie is one of the simplest weight-loss habits. Rotate the recipes across the week so you get different vitamins and flavours, and pair the smoothie with balanced meals the rest of the day.

Do I need protein powder in a weight loss smoothie?

Not required, but helpful. Extra protein keeps you fuller for longer, which matters on a calorie deficit. The book lists a scoop of protein powder as an optional boost on Spinach Meets Cinnamon — or add hemp seeds, chia seeds or nut butter.
