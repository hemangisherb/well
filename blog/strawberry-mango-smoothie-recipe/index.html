Strawberry Mango Smoothie Recipe (Tropical & Fresh)

This strawberry mango smoothie recipe is the tropical breakfast that tastes like dessert and behaves like a proper meal. Frozen mango and strawberries give it a thick, milkshake texture, while soaked oats and almond butter add the fibre and healthy fats that keep you full for hours. It comes from The Ultimate Smoothie Wellness Guide — recipe № 92, Happiness Extract — and it takes five minutes and one blender.

Mango brings the sunshine and strawberries bring the tang, but the clever part is underneath: a creamy almond-milk base, a spoon of acai powder and half a pear, so the sweetness never tips into a sugar crash. At around 400 calories with 11g of protein and 12g of fibre, it is a genuine meal replacement — the kind of breakfast you can drink on a busy morning and forget about until lunch.

The Recipe

5 MINUTES · 1 LARGE GLASS · 1 BLENDER

A bright, easy blend built around mango and strawberries. It is made to keep you full: the creamy base, fibre and healthy fats mean it works as a proper breakfast or a satisfying lunch when time is short.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup almond milk
  • 1–2 tsp acai powder
  • ½ cup frozen mangos
  • ½ fresh pear
  • 2 frozen strawberries
  • ½ cup soaked gluten-free oats
  • 1 tbsp almond butter

Method

  1. Pour in the liquid first, add the greens and soft fresh ingredients, and blend 20–30 seconds until the greens vanish.
  2. Add the frozen fruit, powders, seeds and the rest. Blend until thick and creamy, pausing once to push the mixture down.
  3. Taste, then adjust: more liquid if too thick, more frozen fruit if too thin. Pour and enjoy straight away.
Nutrition per glass (estimated): ~400 cal · Protein 11g · Carbs 60g · Fibre 12g · Fat 15g · Natural sugars ~22g
High in vitamin C · High in fibre · High in calcium

Why It Works

A strawberry mango smoothie earns its place in the Anti-Aging & Glow chapter for a simple reason: vitamin C. Strawberries and mango are both packed with it, and your body needs vitamin C to build collagen — the protein that keeps skin firm and springy. The acai powder adds a deep-purple hit of antioxidants, the compounds that help mop up the free radicals behind dull skin and early lines.

But a glow smoothie that leaves you hungry by mid-morning is no good to anyone, and that is where this recipe is smarter than most. The soaked oats bring soluble fibre that slows digestion and steadies blood sugar, the almond butter adds healthy fats and a little protein, and the pear adds more fibre on top. Together they turn a fruity blend into a meal: roughly 400 calories, 11g of protein and 12g of fibre in one large glass.

It is also a Wellness Herb Pick in the book — one of the recipes we reach for most. Five minutes, one blender, no cooking, and it works just as well as a light lunch when the day runs away from you.

Tips & Variations

Soak the oats the night before: Half a cup of gluten-free oats soaked overnight in water blends completely smooth. Dry oats work in a pinch, but they can leave a slightly gritty texture — soaking is the difference between a smoothie and a milkshake.

Keep the fruit frozen: Frozen mango and frozen strawberries are what make this thick without ice. Ice waters down the flavour; frozen fruit keeps it intense. Keep a bag of each in the freezer and you are two minutes from breakfast.

Make it greener: A handful of spinach disappears completely into this blend — the mango and berries hide both the colour and the taste — and it adds iron and folate for free. Try it once and you will never skip it.

Swap the base: Oat milk makes it even creamier, and peanut butter or sunflower seed butter both work in place of almond butter. If you want it lighter, use half almond milk and half water and drop the nut butter to a teaspoon.

FAQ

Is a strawberry mango smoothie good for breakfast?

Yes — this one is built as a meal replacement. At around 400 calories with 11g of protein and 12g of fibre from oats, pear and almond butter, it keeps you full for hours, unlike a fruit-only smoothie that leaves you hungry by 10am.

Can I use fresh mango instead of frozen mango?

Yes, fresh mango works — just keep the strawberries frozen so the smoothie still turns out thick and cold. If both fruits are fresh, add a small handful of ice cubes and blend a little longer.

Is a strawberry mango smoothie good for skin?

Strawberries and mango are both rich in vitamin C, which your body uses to build collagen, and the acai powder adds antioxidants. It is one of the reasons this blend sits in the Anti-Aging & Glow chapter of The Ultimate Smoothie Wellness Guide.

Can I make this strawberry mango smoothie nut-free?

Yes. Swap the almond milk for oat milk and the almond butter for sunflower seed butter. The texture stays creamy and the flavour stays tropical — the mango and strawberries do the heavy lifting.
