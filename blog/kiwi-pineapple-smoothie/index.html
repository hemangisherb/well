Kiwi Pineapple Smoothie (Vitamin C Boost)

A kiwi pineapple smoothie is the fastest way to turn a grey morning into something bright: tart kiwi, sweet pineapple, and a whole glass of vitamin C in five minutes. This version starts from recipe № 6 in The Ultimate Smoothie Wellness Guide — Pineapple Kale Explosion, from the Immunity & Defence chapter — where pineapple and banana lead and kale blends in without taking over. The book’s blend is pineapple-led, so the recipe card below gives it exactly as printed, and the kiwi twist in Tips & Variations swaps half the pineapple for a peeled kiwi, turning it into a true kiwi-pineapple smoothie.

It is a generous glass at around 485 calories, with 13g of fibre and no added sugar — the sweetness comes only from the fruit, and the soaked pecans or almonds add healthy fats and staying power. The frozen banana makes it thick and cold, the blueberries deepen the colour and the antioxidant count, and a handful of fresh mint (the book’s optional boost) lifts the whole thing. If green smoothies have ever felt like a chore, this is the one to start with: the book says it plainly — pineapple and banana lead, and the kale all but disappears into the fruit.

The Recipe

5 MINUTES · 1 LARGE GLASS · 1 BLENDER

If you like your smoothies fruity rather than grassy, start here: pineapple and banana lead, and kale blends in without taking over. The greens all but disappear into the fruit, which makes this a kind first step if green smoothies still feel new to you. Nothing here is hard to find or tricky to prepare.

Ingredients

  • 1 large frozen banana
  • 3–4 medium leaves of kale, no stem
  • 1 cup fresh pineapple
  • 1 cup fresh blueberries
  • 1 cup filtered water
  • ¼ cup raw soaked pecans or almonds

Optional boost: 1 handful of fresh mint.

Method

  1. Soak-ahead: soak the nuts or seeds listed overnight in filtered water, then drain and rinse.
  2. Pour in the liquid first, add the greens and soft fresh ingredients, and blend 20–30 seconds until the greens vanish.
  3. Add the frozen fruit, powders, seeds and the rest. Blend until thick and creamy, pausing once to push the mixture down.
  4. Taste, then adjust: more liquid if too thick, more frozen fruit if too thin. Pour and enjoy straight away.
Nutrition per glass (estimated): ~485 cal · Protein 8g · Carbs 81g · Fibre 13g · Fat 20g · Natural sugars ~49g
High in vitamin C · High in vitamin K · High in fibre

Why It Works

Kiwi and pineapple are two of the richest everyday sources of vitamin C. A single kiwi carries more vitamin C than an orange, and pineapple adds its own generous dose plus bromelain, the enzyme that gives pineapple its reputation for aiding digestion. Together with a cup of blueberries — one of the most antioxidant-dense fruits you can buy — this glass delivers the vitamin C the book flags with a star: high in vitamin C, high in vitamin K, high in fibre.

The kale is the quiet worker. Three or four leaves add vitamin K, folate and fibre for almost no calories, and because the fruit leads so strongly, you barely taste them — the book calls this a Hidden Green for exactly that reason. The frozen banana does the texture work, turning the blend thick and cold like a milkshake, while the soaked pecans or almonds add healthy fats and a little protein, which is what keeps a fruit smoothie from leaving you hungry an hour later.

At around 485 calories it is a proper light meal rather than a snack — best as breakfast, or as a generous post-workout glass. The fibre (13g) slows the fruit sugars down, so the energy releases steadily instead of spiking and crashing.

Tips & Variations

The kiwi twist: Swap half the pineapple (½ cup) for one peeled kiwi. Kiwi is tart and vivid, and it pushes the vitamin C even higher while keeping the tropical flavour. Use a ripe kiwi — it should give slightly when pressed — and peel it fully, as the skin turns bitter in the blender.

Soak the nuts the night before: The recipe’s first step is a soak-ahead — pecans or almonds in filtered water overnight, then drained and rinsed. Soaking softens them so they blend silky instead of gritty, and it is the one bit of planning this recipe needs. Put them in water before bed and the morning takes five minutes.

Fresh or frozen pineapple: The book uses fresh pineapple, but frozen chunks work just as well and make the smoothie colder and thicker. Use 1 cup straight from the bag, and add a splash more water if it comes out too thick to pour. Frozen pineapple is picked and frozen ripe, so the flavour stays the same.

Add the mint boost: The book suggests a handful of fresh mint, and it is worth trying — it cools the sweetness and makes the whole glass feel brighter, especially with the kiwi twist. Tear the leaves in by hand so they blend in evenly.

Nut-free version: Leave the pecans or almonds out, or swap in a tablespoon of sunflower seeds if you want the richness without tree nuts. The frozen banana keeps the smoothie thick and creamy either way, and skipping the nuts means you can skip the overnight soak entirely.

FAQ

Does this kiwi pineapple smoothie taste like kale?

Barely. Pineapple and banana lead so strongly that the kale all but disappears into the fruit — the book calls this a Hidden Green for exactly that reason. If you want no green taste at all, use a big handful of spinach instead of the kale; it vanishes completely.

Can I use frozen pineapple instead of fresh?

Yes. Use 1 cup of frozen pineapple chunks straight from the bag. The smoothie comes out thicker and colder, so add a splash more water if it is too thick to pour. The flavour stays the same — frozen pineapple is picked and frozen ripe.

Is a kiwi pineapple smoothie good for immunity?

It is rich in vitamin C from the kiwi, pineapple and blueberries, plus vitamin K and fibre from the kale — genuinely useful nutrients. But no single smoothie is a shield on its own: the book puts it well, calling these blends one enjoyable part of a broader pattern of good food, good sleep and fresh air.

Can I make this smoothie without nuts?

Yes. Leave the pecans or almonds out, or swap in a tablespoon of sunflower seeds if you want the richness without tree nuts. The frozen banana keeps the smoothie thick and creamy either way, and you can skip the overnight soak entirely.
